# Project Charter

**Status:** Draft — Phase 0  
**Owner:** Portfolio author  
**Dataset:** Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist (`olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce`)

## Objective

Build a reproducible, recruiter-ready analytical case study that converts e-commerce business questions into validated metrics, reliable analytical datasets, advanced SQL/Python analysis, and a decision-oriented Power BI product.

## Business context

Olist connects customers, sellers, products, payments, fulfillment events, and reviews across a relational order lifecycle. Stakeholders need a consistent view of commercial activity and customer experience while avoiding false conclusions caused by mixed table grains, incomplete lifecycle records, or ambiguous customer identity.

## Target stakeholders

- Executive/commercial leadership: overall demand and trend visibility.
- Sales/category management: category and seller performance.
- Operations/logistics: delivery lead time, lateness, and geographic variation.
- Customer experience: review outcomes and their operational associations.
- Analytics/BI: reusable definitions, model, validation, and reporting assets.

## In scope

- Source inventory, schema and grain validation.
- Data-quality profiling and documented disposition of anomalies.
- Governed commerce, customer, delivery, review, category, seller, and payment metrics.
- A lightweight dimensional model if justified by inspected data.
- Reproducible SQL and Python analysis.
- Power BI-ready datasets and a three-view dashboard design.
- Evidence-linked findings, recommendations, limitations, and reconciliation.

## Out of scope

- Profit, margin, or profitability analysis without validated cost data.
- Causal inference, demand forecasting, production ML, or prescriptive optimization.
- Fabricated/synthetic replacement data or inferred missing source fields.
- Operational deployment, real-time pipelines, personally identifiable enrichment, or external data blending unless separately approved.
- Final findings or dashboard construction before executed analysis.

## Expected deliverables

1. Specifications and decision log.
2. Source/data-quality report and validated data dictionary.
3. Reproducible staging and analytical marts with validation queries.
4. SQL analyses and Python notebooks/figures.
5. Power BI-ready model and dashboard.
6. Executive findings, recommendations, limitations, and reconciliations.
7. Polished GitHub portfolio landing page and case study.

## Portfolio learning objectives

- Demonstrate business-to-technical requirement translation.
- Show grain-aware relational modeling and prevention of double counting.
- Apply metric governance, data-quality reasoning, SQL, Python, and BI design.
- Communicate evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations professionally.

## Constraints and success conditions

The public source is historical and observational. Success means the outputs are reproducible, traceable, internally reconciled, explicitly limited, and understandable to both hiring managers and technical reviewers—not merely visually polished.
